How Big Should a Chicken Coop Be for 3 Chickens?

Buying a prefab instead of building: best chicken coops for 6 hens.

Quick answer: Three standard hens need a 12 sq ft coop and a 30 sq ft run. Twelve is 3 × 4 sq ft, and that 4 sits inside the eXtension range of 3-4 sq ft and the Minnesota Extension range of 3 to 5 sq ft; thirty is 3 × 10 sq ft of outdoor space from eXtension. The indoor minimum of 4.5 to 6 sq ft from Penn State Extension, Maryland Extension, and Utah State Extension is a minimum, not a recommendation.

A 3-hen flock is small enough that the bare minimum and the recommendation can look close until you stand them next to each other: 4.5 sq ft against 12 sq ft. The birds sleep, eat, and lay inside that 12 every night. eXtension also notes that too little space leads to pecking and cannibalism, which is the reason the smaller number stays labeled a floor.

The nest counts do not agree, and that is the useful part of a 3-bird page. One box per 4 to 5 hens rounds to 1 box. Penn State Extension still assigns 2 nests to the first group of 4 hens, and 3 hens are inside that group. Build the 2 if you want the Penn State count; do not average the methods into a number nobody published.

How to size it

Three birds still need a real floor, not a hutch label. Work the rates in order.

  1. Coop floor. 3 × 4 = 12 sq ft. eXtension publishes 3-4 sq ft indoors (3 × 3 = 9 to 3 × 4 = 12). Minnesota Extension publishes 3 to 5 sq ft (3 × 3 = 9 to 3 × 5 = 15). The 4 sq ft figure used on this site sits inside both ranges.
  2. Indoor minimum, not recommended. 3 × 1.5 = 4.5 sq ft and 3 × 2 = 6 sq ft. Penn State Extension lists 1.5 sq ft for laying hens and 2 sq ft for large chickens, Maryland Extension prints the same table, and Utah State Extension lists 1.5 to 2.0 sq ft. Stay above that floor.
  3. Run. 3 × 10 = 30 sq ft from eXtension. Penn State Extension and Maryland Extension also list 10 sq ft of run for large chickens. Their laying-hen run line is 8 sq ft, and 3 × 8 = 24 sq ft.
  4. Nests, two published counts. Minnesota Extension, Utah State Extension, and Maryland Extension say one nest per 4 to 5 hens. One per 5: 3 is under 5, so round up to 1. One per 4: 3 is under 4, so round up to 1. Both ends land on 1 box. 3 hens sit inside the first group of 4, and Penn State Extension assigns 2 nests to that first group, so the count is 2.
  5. Roost length. Utah State Extension 6 to 10 in is 18 in (1 ft 6 in) to 30 in (2 ft 6 in). Penn State Extension 6 in is 18 in (1 ft 6 in). Maryland Extension 6 to 7 in is 18 in (1 ft 6 in) to 21 in (1 ft 9 in). Divide inches by 12 to get feet.
  6. Feeder, nipples, water. Penn State Extension feeder space is at least 3 in, so 3 × 3 = 9 in. Maryland Extension is 2-3 in, so 3 × 2 = 6 to 3 × 3 = 9 in. 3 hens are fewer than the 6 birds on the low end of the Penn State Extension nipple rule (1 nipple per 6-8 birds), so 1 nipple covers this flock. Maryland Extension says a five-gallon waterer can accommodate up to 75 hens, and 3 is under 75, so 1 waterer covers the flock.
  7. Feed. Minnesota Extension says a 6-pound hen eats roughly 3 pounds of feed each week. 3 × 3 = 9 lb per week. That is 9/50 of a 50-lb bag per week.
  8. Example footprint. 12 sq ft is 3 × 4 ft. 30 sq ft is 5 × 6 ft.

Roost length is the easy part of this flock. Utah State Extension 6 to 10 in is 18 in (1 ft 6 in) to 30 in (2 ft 6 in), and a 3 × 4 ft floor has a 3 ft wall, which is longer than 2 ft 6 in. A short offcut covers Utah's upper end.

FAQ

How big should a chicken coop be for 3 chickens?

Give 3 standard hens 12 sq ft of coop and 30 sq ft of run. That is 3 times 4 sq ft indoors and 3 times 10 sq ft outdoors. The indoor 4 sq ft figure sits inside the eXtension 3-4 sq ft range and the Minnesota Extension 3 to 5 sq ft range.

What is the minimum coop for 3 hens?

Penn State lists 1.5 sq ft for laying hens and 2 sq ft for large chickens, Maryland uses that same table, and Utah lists 1.5 to 2.0 sq ft. For 3 hens that is 4.5 to 6 sq ft. Treat it as a minimum, not a recommendation.

How many nesting boxes do 3 hens need?

Minnesota, Utah, and Maryland say one box per 4 to 5 hens, and both 3 divided by 5 and 3 divided by 4 round up to 1 box. Penn State assigns 2 nests to the first 4 hens, and 3 hens are in that first group, so the Penn State count is 2.

How much do 3 hens eat in a week?

Minnesota says a 6-pound hen eats roughly 3 pounds of feed each week, so 3 hens eat 9 pounds a week. That is 9/50 of a 50-lb bag.
