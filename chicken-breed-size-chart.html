Chicken Breed Size & Space Chart

Published adult weights, egg color, cold or heat notes, and laying notes. Thirty-three breeds. Nothing in a cell is estimated.

Large-fowl weights are the cock and hen figures on University of Wisconsin–Madison Extension, Chicken Breeds & Varieties (A2880). That page says the American Poultry Association describes size in the American Standard of Perfection. These rows use the weights printed on the Wisconsin page. They do not add figures from the book. Bantam rows use the mature weights on the linked Livestock Conservancy breed pages. Every figure was copied on October 6, 2026. If that page does not state a fact, the cell says not listed.

The calculator does not read this table. calcCoopSize uses 4 sq ft of coop and 10 sq ft of run for a standard bird, and 2 sq ft of coop and 8 sq ft of run for a bantam. Pick standard or bantam. A heavier standard breed does not get a larger rate.

Worked example

Six standard birds: 6 × 4 = 24 sq ft of coop and 6 × 10 = 60 sq ft of run. calcCoopSize(6, "standard") rounds the coop up to the next 2 sq ft and the run up to the next 4 sq ft. Both products are already on those steps, so the result is 24 and 60. Six bantams: 6 × 2 = 12 sq ft of coop and 6 × 8 = 48 sq ft of run, and the same rounding leaves 12 and 48. Penn State and Maryland list a bantam minimum of 1 sq ft inside and 4 sq ft of run, which is 6 sq ft and 24 sq ft for those six bantams. The calculator's 12 and 48 is extra room above that minimum. Run it on the coop calculator or the flock-size page.

How to use a row

  1. Read the size class. Standard large fowl uses the calculator's standard setting. Bantam uses the bantam setting.
  2. Do not convert the weight into square feet. The Wisconsin page and the Livestock Conservancy pages cited here do not publish coop square feet.
  3. Read hardiness before you plan winter heat. The cold or heat column says not listed when the page does not state cold or heat. A general word such as hardy, with no cold or heat sentence, stays not listed. The winter page keeps Minnesota's 35°F line, Maryland's note on dry adults, and this site's separate 20°F planner.
  4. Read laying as a phrase from that page. A record for one Australorp hen is not a flock average.

FAQ

Does a heavier breed need more coop space in this calculator?

No. calcCoopSize uses 4 sq ft of coop and 10 sq ft of run for every standard bird, and 2 and 8 for every bantam. A Jersey Giant and a Leghorn are both standard. The weights in the table do not change those rates.

Where do the breed weights come from?

Large-fowl rows are the standard cock and hen weights on the University of Wisconsin-Madison Extension page Chicken Breeds & Varieties (A2880), accessed October 6, 2026. Bantam rows are the mature weights on the linked Livestock Conservancy breed pages, accessed the same day. A cell says not listed when that page does not state it.
