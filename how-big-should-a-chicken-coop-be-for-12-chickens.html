How Big Should a Chicken Coop Be for 12 Chickens?

On nests, 12 ÷ 4 = 3 and 12 ÷ 5 leaves a remainder, which still rounds up to 3, so the 4-to-5 rule is 3 boxes. Penn State Extension is 2 for the first 4 plus 8 ÷ 4 = 2 more, which is 4 boxes. The methods differ by one box. Box size, when you cut them: Penn State Extension says nests at least 12 × 12 in, Maryland Extension says one 10 × 10 in box per 4 to 5 chickens, and Utah State Extension says 12-15 in high and wide and at least 12 in deep.

How to size it

Twelve divides cleanly by 4, which is why the two nest methods are only one box apart.

  1. Coop floor. 12 × 4 = 48 sq ft. eXtension publishes 3-4 sq ft indoors (12 × 3 = 36 to 12 × 4 = 48). Minnesota Extension publishes 3 to 5 sq ft (12 × 3 = 36 to 12 × 5 = 60). The 4 sq ft figure used on this site sits inside both ranges.
  2. Indoor minimum, not recommended. 12 × 1.5 = 18 sq ft and 12 × 2 = 24 sq ft. Penn State Extension lists 1.5 sq ft for laying hens and 2 sq ft for large chickens, Maryland Extension prints the same table, and Utah State Extension lists 1.5 to 2.0 sq ft. Stay above that floor.
  3. Run. 12 × 10 = 120 sq ft from eXtension. Penn State Extension and Maryland Extension also list 10 sq ft of run for large chickens. Their laying-hen run line is 8 sq ft, and 12 × 8 = 96 sq ft.
  4. Nests, two published counts. Minnesota Extension, Utah State Extension, and Maryland Extension say one nest per 4 to 5 hens. One per 5: 12 ÷ 5 = 2 with a remainder of 2, so round up to 3. One per 4: 12 ÷ 4 = 3. Both ends land on 3 boxes. Penn State Extension starts with 2 nests for the first 4 hens, then one more per 4 additional hens. 12 − 4 = 8 additional hens, and 8 ÷ 4 = 2, so add 2. The total is 4 nests.
  5. Roost length. Utah State Extension 6 to 10 in is 72 in (6 ft) to 120 in (10 ft). Penn State Extension 6 in is 72 in (6 ft). Maryland Extension 6 to 7 in is 72 in (6 ft) to 84 in (7 ft). Divide inches by 12 to get feet.
  6. Feeder, nipples, water. Penn State Extension feeder space is at least 3 in, so 12 × 3 = 36 in. Maryland Extension is 2-3 in, so 12 × 2 = 24 to 12 × 3 = 36 in. Penn State Extension says 1 nipple per 6-8 birds. At one nipple per 8 birds, 12 ÷ 8 = 1 with a remainder of 4, so round up to 2. At one nipple per 6 birds, 12 ÷ 6 = 2. Plan 2 nipples. Maryland Extension says a five-gallon waterer can accommodate up to 75 hens, and 12 is under 75, so 1 waterer covers the flock.
  7. Feed. Minnesota Extension says a 6-pound hen eats roughly 3 pounds of feed each week. 12 × 3 = 36 lb per week. That is 36/50 of a 50-lb bag per week.
  8. Example footprint. 48 sq ft is 6 × 8 ft. 120 sq ft is 10 × 12 ft.

FAQ

How big should a chicken coop be for 12 chickens?

Lay out 48 sq ft of coop and 120 sq ft of run for 12 standard hens. Multiply 12 by 4 sq ft and 12 by 10 sq ft. The indoor 4 sq ft rate sits inside the eXtension 3-4 sq ft range and the Minnesota Extension 3 to 5 sq ft range.

Why do the nest counts for 12 hens come out 3 and 4?

12 divided by 4 is 3, and 12 divided by 5 rounds up to 3, so one box per 4 to 5 hens is 3 boxes. Penn State is 2 nests for the first 4 hens plus 2 more for the next 8 hens, which is 4 boxes.

How much feeder for 12 hens?

Penn State is at least 3 in per bird, which is 36 in. Maryland is 2-3 in, which is 24 to 36 in. Nipples are 1 per 6-8 birds: 12 divided by 6 is 2, and 12 divided by 8 rounds up to 2, so 2 nipples.

How many hours of light do laying hens need?

Minnesota says at least 12-14 hours of light a day to keep laying. Maryland says 14 hours total light maintains egg production. This 12-hen page does not convert those hours into a bulb wattage.
