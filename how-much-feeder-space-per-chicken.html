How Much Feeder Space Per Chicken?

Full breakdown of what the feed costs, which this page does not calculate: chicken feed cost guide.

Quick answer: Plan at least 3 in of feeder per bird (Penn State Extension), or 2-3 in per laying hen or meat bird (Maryland Extension). Small birds are 1 in and large birds are 3-4 in on the Penn State line. Nipples are 1 per 6-8 birds, and one five-gallon waterer covers up to 75 hens.

Inches of trough and pounds of feed answer different questions. Minnesota Extension says a 6-pound hen eats roughly 3 pounds of feed each week. That weekly weight is how you count bags. It is not a substitute for the 3 in of feeder, and this page does not turn either figure into a price. The cost guide linked above is the place for the bill.

Daily gallons are a separate question from these inches. The water page runs a flock through the feed calculator and then applies the published gallon rates.

A flock can be under the five-gallon limit and still need more than one nipple. Maryland Extension stops the five-gallon waterer at 75 hens. Penn State Extension starts a new nipple every 6-8 birds, so 30 hens are still one waterer and 4 to 5 nipples. Drinker lip space is its own measurement: Maryland Extension wants 2-3 in per bird, the same band as its feeder line, not the nipple count.

Feeder inches are the hen count times 3 (Penn State) or times 2 and times 3 (Maryland). Nipple counts round the count up from hen ÷ 8 and hen ÷ 6. Six hens fall under one nipple because 6 is the start of the 6-8 bird share.

How to size it

Choose the bird size before you multiply. Bantams take the 1 in line. Standard layers take 3 in, or 2-3 in. Large birds take 3-4 in.

  1. Standard feeder. Hens × 3 in (Penn State Extension). Hens × 2 in and hens × 3 in (Maryland Extension). For 10 hens that is 30 in, and 20 to 30 in.
  2. Bantams. Birds × 1 in (Penn State Extension). Ten bantams are 10 in, worked in the breed-size section.
  3. Large birds. Use 3-4 in rather than 3 in (Penn State Extension).
  4. Nipples. Divide the flock by 8 and by 6 and round each result up (Penn State Extension). Ten hens: 10 ÷ 8 = 1 with a remainder of 2, so round up to 2 and 10 ÷ 6 = 1 with a remainder of 4, so round up to 2, so 2 nipples.
  5. Five-gallon waterer. One covers the flock when the head count is 75 or under (Maryland Extension).
  6. Separate areas. Only when the flock is more than 25 birds (Penn State Extension). See the 30-hen example for a flock that crosses the line, and the past-25 section for the count that sits on it.

FAQ

How much feeder space per chicken?

Penn State says at least 3 inches of feeder per bird, 1 linear inch for small birds such as bantams, and 3 to 4 inches for large birds. Maryland says 2 to 3 inches of feeder per laying hen or meat bird.

How much drinker space per chicken?

Maryland says 2 to 3 inches of drinker per bird. Penn State sizes nipple waterers at 1 nipple per 6 to 8 birds. Maryland says a five-gallon waterer can accommodate up to 75 hens.

When should feeders be in separate areas?

Penn State says flocks of more than 25 birds need feeders in separate areas. A flock of 25 is not over that line. A flock of 30 is.

How much feeder for 10 hens?

10 times 3 inches is 30 inches on the Penn State rate. 10 times 2 to 3 inches is 20 to 30 inches on the Maryland rate. Nipples round up to 2, because 10 divided by 8 and 10 divided by 6 both round up to 2.
