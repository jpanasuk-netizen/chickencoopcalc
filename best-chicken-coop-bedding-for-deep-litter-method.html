Best chicken coop bedding for deep litter method

How deep, how much to buy, and what to check every day. No product picks; the extensions name straw and shavings.

Built by Jeremy Panasuk. Published November 17, 2026.

Quick answer: University of Minnesota Extension says to bed a coop deeply with 4 to 6 inches of straw or shavings, stirring as the top layer soils and adding fresh bedding on top, and to fit a 6 to 8 inch kickboard at the doorway. Kansas State Research and Extension warns deep bedding raises humidity, so check moisture daily.

Litter volume calculator

Enter the coop floor and a bedding depth. The result is the loose volume to spread, in cubic feet: length × width × depth ÷ 12. That is plain arithmetic on this page. Bag and bale sizes vary, so divide by the volume printed on what you buy.

Default floor is 6 × 4 ft, a 24 sq ft coop like the one in the heat lamp guide's worked example; change it to yours. Floor area per bird is on chicken coop size by flock.

How deep: three extensions, two ranges

SourceDepthMaterialWhat it says the depth does
University of Minnesota Extension4 to 6 in.Straw or shavingsDeep bedding gives some natural heat as manure decomposes; needs a deep cleaning in spring
University of Maryland Extension, FS-11333 to 4 in.Pine shavings or strawGood insulation; deep litter also generates heat as microbes break it down
K-State Research and Extension, Douglas CountyNo depth given—Deep bedding increases humidity; check moisture daily

The two depth ranges overlap at 4 inches. They are listed here as each extension states them, not averaged. The calculator offers 3 to 6 inches so you can run either one.

Daily moisture check

  • Check the moisture level in the coop every day (K-State).
  • As the top layer soils, stir the bedding so manure moves to the bottom, then add fresh bedding on top (Minnesota).
  • If the litter is damp, clean more often or adjust, mix or fluff it to keep it warm and dry (K-State).
  • Remove wet, frozen or humid litter to protect feet from frostbite (K-State).
  • Plan a deep clean-out in spring (Minnesota).
