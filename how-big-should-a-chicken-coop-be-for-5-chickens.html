How Big Should a Chicken Coop Be for 5 Chickens?

Full breakdown for the next head count: how big a chicken coop should be for 6 hens. Prefab shopping sits on best chicken coops for 6 hens.

Quick answer: Five hens need 20 sq ft of coop and 50 sq ft of run. That is 5 × 4 sq ft and 5 × 10 sq ft, with the 4 drawn from inside the eXtension 3-4 sq ft range and the Minnesota Extension 3 to 5 sq ft range. The indoor minimum of 7.5 to 10 sq ft is a minimum, not a recommendation.

How to size it

Five is the awkward count between a square 4-hen floor and the separate 6-hen page. This page stays on 5.

  1. Coop floor. 5 × 4 = 20 sq ft. eXtension publishes 3-4 sq ft indoors (5 × 3 = 15 to 5 × 4 = 20). Minnesota Extension publishes 3 to 5 sq ft (5 × 3 = 15 to 5 × 5 = 25). The 4 sq ft figure used on this site sits inside both ranges.
  2. Indoor minimum, not recommended. 5 × 1.5 = 7.5 sq ft and 5 × 2 = 10 sq ft. Penn State Extension lists 1.5 sq ft for laying hens and 2 sq ft for large chickens, Maryland Extension prints the same table, and Utah State Extension lists 1.5 to 2.0 sq ft. Stay above that floor.
  3. Run. 5 × 10 = 50 sq ft from eXtension. Penn State Extension and Maryland Extension also list 10 sq ft of run for large chickens. Their laying-hen run line is 8 sq ft, and 5 × 8 = 40 sq ft.
  4. Nests, two published counts. Minnesota Extension, Utah State Extension, and Maryland Extension say one nest per 4 to 5 hens. One per 5: 5 ÷ 5 = 1. One per 4: 5 ÷ 4 = 1 with a remainder of 1, so round up to 2. The range is 1 to 2 boxes. Penn State Extension starts with 2 nests for the first 4 hens, then one more per 4 additional hens. 5 − 4 = 1 additional hen, and 1 hen is fewer than 4, so add 1. The total is 3 nests.
  5. Roost length. Utah State Extension 6 to 10 in is 30 in (2 ft 6 in) to 50 in (4 ft 2 in). Penn State Extension 6 in is 30 in (2 ft 6 in). Maryland Extension 6 to 7 in is 30 in (2 ft 6 in) to 35 in (2 ft 11 in). Divide inches by 12 to get feet.
  6. Feeder, nipples, water. Penn State Extension feeder space is at least 3 in, so 5 × 3 = 15 in. Maryland Extension is 2-3 in, so 5 × 2 = 10 to 5 × 3 = 15 in. 5 hens are fewer than the 6 birds on the low end of the Penn State Extension nipple rule (1 nipple per 6-8 birds), so 1 nipple covers this flock. Maryland Extension says a five-gallon waterer can accommodate up to 75 hens, and 5 is under 75, so 1 waterer covers the flock.
  7. Feed. Minnesota Extension says a 6-pound hen eats roughly 3 pounds of feed each week. 5 × 3 = 15 lb per week. That is 15/50 of a 50-lb bag per week.
  8. Example footprint. 20 sq ft is 4 × 5 ft. 50 sq ft is 5 × 10 ft.

FAQ

How big should a chicken coop be for 5 chickens?

Size 5 standard hens at 20 sq ft of coop and 50 sq ft of run. That is 5 times 4 sq ft and 5 times 10 sq ft. The indoor 4 sq ft rate sits inside the eXtension 3-4 sq ft range and the Minnesota Extension 3 to 5 sq ft range.

What indoor minimum applies to 5 hens?

Multiply 5 by 1.5 and by 2. That is 7.5 to 10 sq ft, from the Penn State and Maryland laying-hen and large-chicken lines and from Utah's 1.5 to 2.0 sq ft. It is a minimum, not a recommendation.

How many nests do 5 hens need?

One per 5 hens is 1 box. One per 4 hens rounds 5 divided by 4 up to 2 boxes. Penn State is 2 nests for the first 4 hens plus 1 more for the single additional hen, which is 3 nests.

What footprint equals 20 sq ft?

A 4 by 5 ft floor is 20 sq ft. A 5 by 10 ft run is 50 sq ft. Other side lengths that multiply to the same areas are the same floors.
