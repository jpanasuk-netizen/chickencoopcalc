How big should a chicken coop be for 6 hens?

24 sq ft inside, 60 sq ft of run, up to 5 feet of roost bar, 2 nesting boxes.

Quick answer: Six hens at 4 sq ft inside and 10 sq ft of run need a 24 sq ft coop (about 4×6 ft) and a 60 sq ft run. The 4 sits in the eXtension and UC Master Gardener range of about 3–4 sq ft indoors, and 10 sq ft outdoors is the figure both pages publish.

The formula from the extension services

Coop: 4 sq ft per standard bird. Run: 10 sq ft per bird. Six hens → 24 sq ft of coop floor and 60 sq ft of run. Penn State and Maryland list a lower indoor minimum (1.5 sq ft for laying hens, 2 for large chickens). Their bantam row is 1 sq ft inside and 4 sq ft of run, not half of 4 and 10.

eXtension says too little space can lead to pecking. The 4 sq ft figure is the top of its 3–4 sq ft indoor minimum, not a lower floor from the Penn State table. Give more than a cramped coop.

The prefab-label trap

Coops marketed as "fits 6–8 chickens" routinely measure 12–16 sq ft inside — about half of 6 × 4 sq ft. The fix is mechanical: read the interior dimensions and divide by 4 to see how many birds that floor holds at this site's planning rate. The best coops for 6 hens guide applies that test to current prefab listings.

Run size when chickens free-range

Birds are locked in every night, so the indoor 4 sq ft rate still applies. The pages cited here do not publish a 5–8 sq ft free-range run. eXtension and the UC handout still say about 10 sq ft outdoors, and Penn State and Maryland list 8 sq ft of run for laying hens. A 60 sq ft run is the 10 sq ft rate for six hens.

Sizing up on purpose

Three reasons real builders land above the minimum:

  • Chicken math is real. Almost everyone who starts with 6 ends up with 8–10 within two years. Building 32 sq ft now costs less than adding later.
  • Winter floor space shrinks. Deep-litter bedding, feeders, and the waterer all live indoors December–March — 24 sq ft of open floor becomes ~20 fast.
  • Integration space. New birds need room to escape the established pecking order; cramped introductions turn bloody.

A 4×8 ft shed-style coop (32 sq ft) with a 6×12 run is the sweet-spot six-hen build: above minimum, standard lumber sizes, one sheet-goods-friendly footprint.

FAQ

How big should a coop be for 6 hens?

24 sq ft inside (4 sq ft/bird, inside the published 3–4 sq ft range) plus a 60 sq ft run (10 sq ft/bird), with 3–5 ft of roost (Utah's 6–10 inches) and 2 nesting boxes (one per 4 to 5 hens). That's roughly a 4×6 coop on a 6×10 run.

Can 6 chickens live in a small prefab coop?

Almost none sold as "6–8 bird" coops actually offer 24 sq ft inside — most are 12–16. Divide the listed interior dimensions by 4 to get the true capacity. Real six-hen prefabs run $600–1,200.

Do bantams need less space?

Penn State and Maryland list 1 sq ft inside and 4 sq ft of run for bantams. That is the published minimum, not 2 and 8. Six bantams at that minimum are 6 sq ft inside and 24 sq ft of run. The calculator's 2 and 8 rate is extra room above those rows. Penn State roost space for bantams is 4 inches, not the standard-chicken 6 inches.
