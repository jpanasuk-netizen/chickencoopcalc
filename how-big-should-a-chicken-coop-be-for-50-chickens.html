How Big Should a Chicken Coop Be for 50 Chickens?

Quick answer: Fifty hens need 200 sq ft indoors and 500 sq ft of run, from 50 × 4 and 50 × 10. One 40-watt bulb covers about 200 sq ft on the Maryland Extension lighting note, which matches this floor. Feeders go in separate areas because 50 is more than 25 birds (Penn State Extension).

How to size it

Fifty is a lighting problem and a feeder-layout problem on top of the usual multiplication.

  1. Coop floor. 50 × 4 = 200 sq ft. eXtension publishes 3-4 sq ft indoors (50 × 3 = 150 to 50 × 4 = 200). Minnesota Extension publishes 3 to 5 sq ft (50 × 3 = 150 to 50 × 5 = 250). The 4 sq ft figure used on this site sits inside both ranges.
  2. Indoor minimum, not recommended. 50 × 1.5 = 75 sq ft and 50 × 2 = 100 sq ft. Penn State Extension lists 1.5 sq ft for laying hens and 2 sq ft for large chickens, Maryland Extension prints the same table, and Utah State Extension lists 1.5 to 2.0 sq ft. Stay above that floor.
  3. Run. 50 × 10 = 500 sq ft from eXtension. Penn State Extension and Maryland Extension also list 10 sq ft of run for large chickens. Their laying-hen run line is 8 sq ft, and 50 × 8 = 400 sq ft.
  4. Nests, two published counts. Minnesota Extension, Utah State Extension, and Maryland Extension say one nest per 4 to 5 hens. One per 5: 50 ÷ 5 = 10. One per 4: 50 ÷ 4 = 12 with a remainder of 2, so round up to 13. The range is 10 to 13 boxes. Penn State Extension starts with 2 nests for the first 4 hens, then one more per 4 additional hens. 50 − 4 = 46 additional hens, and 46 ÷ 4 = 11 with a remainder of 2, so add 12. The total is 14 nests.
  5. Roost length. Utah State Extension 6 to 10 in is 300 in (25 ft) to 500 in (41 ft 8 in). Penn State Extension 6 in is 300 in (25 ft). Maryland Extension 6 to 7 in is 300 in (25 ft) to 350 in (29 ft 2 in). Divide inches by 12 to get feet.
  6. Feeder, nipples, water. Penn State Extension feeder space is at least 3 in, so 50 × 3 = 150 in. Maryland Extension is 2-3 in, so 50 × 2 = 100 to 50 × 3 = 150 in. Penn State Extension says 1 nipple per 6-8 birds. At one nipple per 8 birds, 50 ÷ 8 = 6 with a remainder of 2, so round up to 7. At one nipple per 6 birds, 50 ÷ 6 = 8 with a remainder of 2, so round up to 9. Plan 7 to 9 nipples. Maryland Extension says a five-gallon waterer can accommodate up to 75 hens, and 50 is under 75, so 1 waterer covers the flock.
  7. Feed. Minnesota Extension says a 6-pound hen eats roughly 3 pounds of feed each week. 50 × 3 = 150 lb per week. 150 ÷ 50 = 3 bags of 50 lb per week.
  8. Example footprint. 200 sq ft is 10 × 20 ft. 500 sq ft is 20 × 25 ft.

Lighting for this 200 sq ft floor

Maryland Extension says one 25-watt bulb lights 40 sq ft, so 200 ÷ 40 = 5 of those bulbs cover 200 sq ft. The same source says one 40-watt bulb lights about 200 sq ft, so 1 bulb of that size matches this coop. Penn State Extension says one low-intensity bulb (40 watts or less) every 40 ft at ceiling height, and that a bird needs only 1 foot-candle for year-round production. A 10 × 20 ft example of the 200 sq ft floor is 20 ft on the long side, and 20 is less than 40, so one bulb meets that spacing on a house of this footprint.

Minnesota Extension says hens need at least 12-14 hours of light a day to keep laying. Maryland Extension says 14 hours total light maintains egg production. Heat lamps are a different question from this daylight fill: the winter heat-lamp guide is the longer page on winter heat.

FAQ

How big should a chicken coop be for 50 chickens?

Start from 200 sq ft of coop and 500 sq ft of run for 50 standard hens. That is 50 times 4 sq ft and 50 times 10 sq ft. The indoor 4 sq ft rate sits inside the eXtension 3-4 sq ft range and the Minnesota Extension 3 to 5 sq ft range.

What light does a 200 sq ft coop need?

Maryland says one 40-watt bulb lights about 200 sq ft, so one bulb of that size matches a 200 sq ft floor. The same source says one 25-watt bulb lights 40 sq ft, and 200 divided by 40 is 5 of those bulbs. Penn State says one bulb of 40 watts or less every 40 ft, and that a bird needs only 1 foot-candle.

How many 50-lb bags do 50 hens eat in a week?

Minnesota's rate is about 3 lb a week for a 6-pound hen. 50 times 3 is 150 lb, and 150 divided by 50 is 3 bags a week.

Do 50 hens still share one five-gallon waterer?

Yes. Maryland says a five-gallon waterer can accommodate up to 75 hens, and 50 is under 75. Nipple drinkers are separate: 50 divided by 8 rounds up to 7, and 50 divided by 6 rounds up to 9, so plan 7 to 9 nipples. Feeders go in separate areas because 50 is more than 25 birds.
