How Big Should a Chicken Coop Be for 10 Bantams?

Standard hens of the same count are a different floor: coop size for 10 chickens uses 40 sq ft indoors. That 40 sq ft number is not the bantam minimum.

Quick answer: Ten bantams need at least 10 sq ft indoors and 40 sq ft of run. Penn State Extension and Maryland Extension list bantam chickens at 1 sq ft inside and 4 sq ft of run, and both figures are minimums on that table. These sources do not publish a larger bantam allowance, so treat 10 and 40 as the floor.

The standard-hen pages on this site use 4 sq ft indoors because that number sits inside the eXtension 3-4 sq ft range and the Minnesota Extension 3 to 5 sq ft range. Bantams are not given that 4 sq ft in these sources. The only bantam floor figures here are the 1 and the 4 on the Penn State Extension and Maryland Extension minimum table. Building larger than 10 sq ft is a choice you make without a second published bantam rate to multiply.

Roost height is the other place a standard plan will be wrong. Penn State Extension puts bantam perches 12-18 in off the floor and standard perches 18-24 in, and it caps every perch at 4 ft. A 40 in bar (3 ft 4 in) at the bantam height is the length. Do not also apply the Utah 6-10 in standard-chicken perch to this flock; that range is not the bantam line.

How to size it

Stay on the bantam row of the table. The laying-hen 1.5 sq ft line is for standard layers, not for these birds.

  1. Indoor minimum. 10 × 1 = 10 sq ft (Penn State Extension, Maryland Extension).
  2. Run minimum. 10 × 4 = 40 sq ft (Penn State Extension, Maryland Extension).
  3. Roost. 10 × 4 in = 40 in, which is 3 ft 4 in, set 12-18 in above the floor (Penn State Extension).
  4. Feeder. 10 × 1 in = 10 in for small birds (Penn State Extension).
  5. Nests. Minnesota Extension, Utah State Extension, and Maryland Extension say one nest per 4 to 5 hens. One per 5: 10 ÷ 5 = 2. One per 4: 10 ÷ 4 = 2 with a remainder of 2, so round up to 3. The range is 2 to 3 boxes. Penn State Extension starts with 2 nests for the first 4 hens, then one more per 4 additional hens. 10 − 4 = 6 additional hens, and 6 ÷ 4 = 1 with a remainder of 2, so add 2. The total is 4 nests. The sources do not print a different ratio for bantams.
  6. Example footprint. 10 sq ft is 2 × 5 ft. 40 sq ft is 5 × 8 ft. Both products are exact. Another pair of sides with the same area is the same floor.

FAQ

How big should a chicken coop be for 10 bantams?

Penn State and Maryland list bantam chickens at 1 sq ft inside and 4 sq ft of run, both as minimums. For 10 bantams that is 10 sq ft indoors and 40 sq ft of run. These sources do not publish a higher bantam allowance.

How much roost do 10 bantams need?

Penn State gives bantams 4 in of perch and places the bar 12-18 in above the floor. 10 times 4 in is 40 in, which is 3 ft 4 in. Standard chickens on the same page are 6 in and 18-24 in above the floor, so do not reuse the standard height.

How much feeder space do 10 bantams need?

Penn State lists 1 linear inch per small bird. 10 times 1 in is 10 in. The same source lists 3-4 in for large birds, which is the other row, not the bantam row.

How many nests for 10 bantams?

The sources do not publish a separate bantam nest ratio. One per 4 to 5 hens is 2 to 3 boxes. Penn State's count is 2 for the first 4 birds plus 2 more for the next 6, which is 4 nests.
