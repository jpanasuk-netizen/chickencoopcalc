How Big Should a Chicken Coop Be for 10 Chickens?

Quick answer: Ten standard hens need a 40 sq ft coop and a 100 sq ft run. Forty is 10 × 4 sq ft, and that 4 sits inside the eXtension allowance of 3-4 sq ft per hen and the Minnesota Extension allowance of 3 to 5 sq ft. One hundred is 10 × 10 sq ft of run from eXtension.

How to size it

Ten is the round flock: the coop and the run both end in zeros, and the example floor is the 5 × 8 people sketch first.

  1. Coop floor. 10 × 4 = 40 sq ft. eXtension publishes 3-4 sq ft indoors (10 × 3 = 30 to 10 × 4 = 40). Minnesota Extension publishes 3 to 5 sq ft (10 × 3 = 30 to 10 × 5 = 50). The 4 sq ft figure used on this site sits inside both ranges.
  2. Indoor minimum, not recommended. 10 × 1.5 = 15 sq ft and 10 × 2 = 20 sq ft. Penn State Extension lists 1.5 sq ft for laying hens and 2 sq ft for large chickens, Maryland Extension prints the same table, and Utah State Extension lists 1.5 to 2.0 sq ft. Stay above that floor.
  3. Run. 10 × 10 = 100 sq ft from eXtension. Penn State Extension and Maryland Extension also list 10 sq ft of run for large chickens. Their laying-hen run line is 8 sq ft, and 10 × 8 = 80 sq ft.
  4. Nests, two published counts. Minnesota Extension, Utah State Extension, and Maryland Extension say one nest per 4 to 5 hens. One per 5: 10 ÷ 5 = 2. One per 4: 10 ÷ 4 = 2 with a remainder of 2, so round up to 3. The range is 2 to 3 boxes. Penn State Extension starts with 2 nests for the first 4 hens, then one more per 4 additional hens. 10 − 4 = 6 additional hens, and 6 ÷ 4 = 1 with a remainder of 2, so add 2. The total is 4 nests.
  5. Roost length. Utah State Extension 6 to 10 in is 60 in (5 ft) to 100 in (8 ft 4 in). Penn State Extension 6 in is 60 in (5 ft). Maryland Extension 6 to 7 in is 60 in (5 ft) to 70 in (5 ft 10 in). Divide inches by 12 to get feet.
  6. Feeder, nipples, water. Penn State Extension feeder space is at least 3 in, so 10 × 3 = 30 in. Maryland Extension is 2-3 in, so 10 × 2 = 20 to 10 × 3 = 30 in. Penn State Extension says 1 nipple per 6-8 birds. At one nipple per 8 birds, 10 ÷ 8 = 1 with a remainder of 2, so round up to 2. At one nipple per 6 birds, 10 ÷ 6 = 1 with a remainder of 4, so round up to 2. Plan 2 nipples. Maryland Extension says a five-gallon waterer can accommodate up to 75 hens, and 10 is under 75, so 1 waterer covers the flock.
  7. Feed. Minnesota Extension says a 6-pound hen eats roughly 3 pounds of feed each week. 10 × 3 = 30 lb per week. That is 30/50 of a 50-lb bag per week.
  8. Example footprint. 40 sq ft is 5 × 8 ft. 100 sq ft is 10 × 10 ft.

FAQ

How big should a chicken coop be for 10 chickens?

Use 40 sq ft of coop and 100 sq ft of run for 10 standard hens. That is 10 times 4 sq ft indoors and 10 times 10 sq ft outdoors. The indoor 4 sq ft figure sits inside the eXtension 3-4 sq ft range and the Minnesota Extension 3 to 5 sq ft range.

What is the smallest indoor figure for 10 hens?

10 times 1.5 is 15 sq ft and 10 times 2 is 20 sq ft. Penn State and Maryland list 1.5 for laying hens and 2 for large chickens, and Utah lists 1.5 to 2.0. Call 15 to 20 sq ft a minimum, not a recommendation.

How many nesting boxes for 10 hens?

One per 5 hens is 2 boxes. One per 4 hens rounds 10 divided by 4 up to 3 boxes. Penn State is 2 nests for the first 4, plus 2 more for the 6 additional hens, which is 4 nests.

How many eggs a week does Minnesota's rate imply for 10 hens?

Minnesota says roughly 6 eggs per week. 10 times 6 is 60 eggs a week. The same source says hens need at least 12-14 hours of light a day to keep laying.
