How Big Should a Chicken Coop Be for 30 Chickens?

Quick answer: Thirty hens need 120 sq ft of coop and 300 sq ft of run. The feeder rule changes at this count: Penn State Extension says that with more than 25 birds, put feeders in separate areas, and 30 is over 25. The floor is still 30 × 4 sq ft and the run is still 30 × 10 sq ft.

How to size it

Thirty is the first page in this set where the feeder is no longer one line of trough.

  1. Coop floor. 30 × 4 = 120 sq ft. eXtension publishes 3-4 sq ft indoors (30 × 3 = 90 to 30 × 4 = 120). Minnesota Extension publishes 3 to 5 sq ft (30 × 3 = 90 to 30 × 5 = 150). The 4 sq ft figure used on this site sits inside both ranges.
  2. Indoor minimum, not recommended. 30 × 1.5 = 45 sq ft and 30 × 2 = 60 sq ft. Penn State Extension lists 1.5 sq ft for laying hens and 2 sq ft for large chickens, Maryland Extension prints the same table, and Utah State Extension lists 1.5 to 2.0 sq ft. Stay above that floor.
  3. Run. 30 × 10 = 300 sq ft from eXtension. Penn State Extension and Maryland Extension also list 10 sq ft of run for large chickens. Their laying-hen run line is 8 sq ft, and 30 × 8 = 240 sq ft.
  4. Nests, two published counts. Minnesota Extension, Utah State Extension, and Maryland Extension say one nest per 4 to 5 hens. One per 5: 30 ÷ 5 = 6. One per 4: 30 ÷ 4 = 7 with a remainder of 2, so round up to 8. The range is 6 to 8 boxes. Penn State Extension starts with 2 nests for the first 4 hens, then one more per 4 additional hens. 30 − 4 = 26 additional hens, and 26 ÷ 4 = 6 with a remainder of 2, so add 7. The total is 9 nests.
  5. Roost length. Utah State Extension 6 to 10 in is 180 in (15 ft) to 300 in (25 ft). Penn State Extension 6 in is 180 in (15 ft). Maryland Extension 6 to 7 in is 180 in (15 ft) to 210 in (17 ft 6 in). Divide inches by 12 to get feet.
  6. Feeder, nipples, water. Penn State Extension feeder space is at least 3 in, so 30 × 3 = 90 in. Maryland Extension is 2-3 in, so 30 × 2 = 60 to 30 × 3 = 90 in. Penn State Extension says 1 nipple per 6-8 birds. At one nipple per 8 birds, 30 ÷ 8 = 3 with a remainder of 6, so round up to 4. At one nipple per 6 birds, 30 ÷ 6 = 5. Plan 4 to 5 nipples. Maryland Extension says a five-gallon waterer can accommodate up to 75 hens, and 30 is under 75, so 1 waterer covers the flock.
  7. Feed. Minnesota Extension says a 6-pound hen eats roughly 3 pounds of feed each week. 30 × 3 = 90 lb per week. That is one 50-lb bag plus 40 lb (90/50 bags) per week.
  8. Example footprint. 120 sq ft is 10 × 12 ft. 300 sq ft is 15 × 20 ft.

FAQ

How big should a chicken coop be for 30 chickens?

Count 120 sq ft of coop and 300 sq ft of run for 30 standard hens. That is 30 times 4 sq ft and 30 times 10 sq ft. The indoor 4 sq ft rate sits inside the eXtension 3-4 sq ft range and the Minnesota Extension 3 to 5 sq ft range.

Where do the feeders go for 30 hens?

Penn State says flocks of more than 25 birds need feeders in separate areas. 30 is over 25. Penn State feeder length is 90 in and Maryland is 60 to 90 in, split across those areas.

How many nipple drinkers for 30 hens?

Penn State is 1 nipple per 6-8 birds. 30 divided by 6 is 5. 30 divided by 8 rounds up to 4. Plan 4 to 5 nipples. A five-gallon waterer still covers 30 hens because Maryland's limit is 75.

How many nesting boxes for 30 hens?

30 divided by 5 is 6 boxes and 30 divided by 4 rounds up to 8, so the 4-to-5 rule is 6 to 8 boxes. Penn State is 2 for the first 4 hens plus 7 more, which is 9 nests.
