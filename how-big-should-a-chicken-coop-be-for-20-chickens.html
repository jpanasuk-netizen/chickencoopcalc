How Big Should a Chicken Coop Be for 20 Chickens?

Quick answer: Twenty hens need 80 sq ft of coop and 200 sq ft of run. Eighty is 20 × 4 sq ft and 200 is 20 × 10 sq ft. An example 8 × 10 ft floor is the 80 sq ft, which is a walk-in footprint rather than a low box.

How to size it

Twenty is where the weekly feed passes a full 50-lb bag and the house is easier to walk into than to reach into.

  1. Coop floor. 20 × 4 = 80 sq ft. eXtension publishes 3-4 sq ft indoors (20 × 3 = 60 to 20 × 4 = 80). Minnesota Extension publishes 3 to 5 sq ft (20 × 3 = 60 to 20 × 5 = 100). The 4 sq ft figure used on this site sits inside both ranges.
  2. Indoor minimum, not recommended. 20 × 1.5 = 30 sq ft and 20 × 2 = 40 sq ft. Penn State Extension lists 1.5 sq ft for laying hens and 2 sq ft for large chickens, Maryland Extension prints the same table, and Utah State Extension lists 1.5 to 2.0 sq ft. Stay above that floor.
  3. Run. 20 × 10 = 200 sq ft from eXtension. Penn State Extension and Maryland Extension also list 10 sq ft of run for large chickens. Their laying-hen run line is 8 sq ft, and 20 × 8 = 160 sq ft.
  4. Nests, two published counts. Minnesota Extension, Utah State Extension, and Maryland Extension say one nest per 4 to 5 hens. One per 5: 20 ÷ 5 = 4. One per 4: 20 ÷ 4 = 5. The range is 4 to 5 boxes. Penn State Extension starts with 2 nests for the first 4 hens, then one more per 4 additional hens. 20 − 4 = 16 additional hens, and 16 ÷ 4 = 4, so add 4. The total is 6 nests.
  5. Roost length. Utah State Extension 6 to 10 in is 120 in (10 ft) to 200 in (16 ft 8 in). Penn State Extension 6 in is 120 in (10 ft). Maryland Extension 6 to 7 in is 120 in (10 ft) to 140 in (11 ft 8 in). Divide inches by 12 to get feet.
  6. Feeder, nipples, water. Penn State Extension feeder space is at least 3 in, so 20 × 3 = 60 in. Maryland Extension is 2-3 in, so 20 × 2 = 40 to 20 × 3 = 60 in. Penn State Extension says 1 nipple per 6-8 birds. At one nipple per 8 birds, 20 ÷ 8 = 2 with a remainder of 4, so round up to 3. At one nipple per 6 birds, 20 ÷ 6 = 3 with a remainder of 2, so round up to 4. Plan 3 to 4 nipples. Maryland Extension says a five-gallon waterer can accommodate up to 75 hens, and 20 is under 75, so 1 waterer covers the flock.
  7. Feed. Minnesota Extension says a 6-pound hen eats roughly 3 pounds of feed each week. 20 × 3 = 60 lb per week. That is one 50-lb bag plus 10 lb (60/50 bags) per week.
  8. Example footprint. 80 sq ft is 8 × 10 ft. 200 sq ft is 10 × 20 ft.

For the 200 sq ft run, Penn State Extension says to bury run wire at least 12 in deep and toe it outward about 6 in. Maryland Extension says to use 1/2-inch hardware cloth over windows.

FAQ

How big should a chicken coop be for 20 chickens?

Set 80 sq ft of coop and 200 sq ft of run for 20 standard hens. That is 20 times 4 sq ft and 20 times 10 sq ft. The indoor 4 sq ft rate sits inside the eXtension 3-4 sq ft range and the Minnesota Extension 3 to 5 sq ft range.

How much feed do 20 hens eat per week?

Minnesota says a 6-pound hen eats roughly 3 pounds a week, so 20 times 3 is 60 lb a week. That is one 50-lb bag plus 10 lb.

Do 20 hens need feeders in separate areas?

No. Penn State says to put feeders in separate areas when there are more than 25 birds. A flock of 20 is under that line.

How long is the roost for 20 hens?

Penn State at 6 in is 120 in, or 10 ft. Utah at 6 to 10 in is 120 to 200 in, or 10 ft to 16 ft 8 in. Maryland at 6 to 7 in is 120 to 140 in, or 10 ft to 11 ft 8 in.
