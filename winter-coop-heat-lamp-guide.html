Do chickens need a heat lamp in winter?

These sources do not set an adult heat cutoff. The numbered temperature schedule is for the brooder.

Quick answer: The housing pages cited here do not set an adult heat-lamp cutoff. They do treat damp, stale air as the winter problem. Maryland Extension's numbered schedule is for chicks: 90–95°F the first week, then 5°F less each week. The vent areas below are this site's planning ratio of about 1 sq ft of high opening per 10 sq ft of floor, not a published formula.

Moisture is the winter problem these pages describe

The housing pages cited here treat damp, poorly ventilated air as the winter problem, not a missing heat lamp. Sealing a coop traps moisture from breath and droppings. A heat lamp is a fire risk; these pages do not count coop fires against cold losses, and they do not publish an adult temperature such as −10°F or −20°F. If the power fails, birds that were depending on a lamp lose that heat at once.

Maryland says to use 1/2-inch hardware cloth over windows. Put vents above roost height and out of the wind. Condensation on the glass in the morning means the air is too wet. Lighting figures that Maryland does publish — one 25-watt bulb for 40 square feet, or one 40-watt bulb for 200 square feet — are on the flock-size page, in the 200 sq ft lighting section.

If you still add a lamp

These sources do not list an adult cutoff such as −20°F or a wattage such as 250 W per 16 sq ft. If you still add heat for a breed that cannot take cold, or for a bird that is ill, Maryland's clearance for a heat lamp is at least 18 inches above the floor. Keep the lamp secured and the coop dry. A warm dry coop is not a number these pages publish.

Brooder math (this is where lamps are required)

Maryland Extension: start chicks at 90–95°F the first week, then reduce 5°F each week until they are five weeks old and the room is about 70–75°F. The lamp should be at least 18 inches above the floor. Watch the chicks: panting and crowding away from the lamp means too hot; a tight huddle under the lamp means too cold. These pages do not assign a 250 W bulb to a 4×4 ft brooder or a 100 W bulb to a 2×2 ft brooder.
