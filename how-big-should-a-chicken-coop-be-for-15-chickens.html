How Big Should a Chicken Coop Be for 15 Chickens?

Quick answer: Fifteen hens need 60 sq ft indoors and 150 sq ft of run, from 15 × 4 and 15 × 10. Keep the indoor minimum of 22.5 to 30 sq ft labeled a minimum, not a recommendation. The 4 sq ft rate sits inside eXtension 3-4 sq ft and Minnesota Extension 3 to 5 sq ft.

How to size it

At 15 hens the roost, not the floor multiplication, is the piece that stops fitting on one wall.

  1. Coop floor. 15 × 4 = 60 sq ft. eXtension publishes 3-4 sq ft indoors (15 × 3 = 45 to 15 × 4 = 60). Minnesota Extension publishes 3 to 5 sq ft (15 × 3 = 45 to 15 × 5 = 75). The 4 sq ft figure used on this site sits inside both ranges.
  2. Indoor minimum, not recommended. 15 × 1.5 = 22.5 sq ft and 15 × 2 = 30 sq ft. Penn State Extension lists 1.5 sq ft for laying hens and 2 sq ft for large chickens, Maryland Extension prints the same table, and Utah State Extension lists 1.5 to 2.0 sq ft. Stay above that floor.
  3. Run. 15 × 10 = 150 sq ft from eXtension. Penn State Extension and Maryland Extension also list 10 sq ft of run for large chickens. Their laying-hen run line is 8 sq ft, and 15 × 8 = 120 sq ft.
  4. Nests, two published counts. Minnesota Extension, Utah State Extension, and Maryland Extension say one nest per 4 to 5 hens. One per 5: 15 ÷ 5 = 3. One per 4: 15 ÷ 4 = 3 with a remainder of 3, so round up to 4. The range is 3 to 4 boxes. Penn State Extension starts with 2 nests for the first 4 hens, then one more per 4 additional hens. 15 − 4 = 11 additional hens, and 11 ÷ 4 = 2 with a remainder of 3, so add 3. The total is 5 nests.
  5. Roost length. Utah State Extension 6 to 10 in is 90 in (7 ft 6 in) to 150 in (12 ft 6 in). Penn State Extension 6 in is 90 in (7 ft 6 in). Maryland Extension 6 to 7 in is 90 in (7 ft 6 in) to 105 in (8 ft 9 in). Divide inches by 12 to get feet.
  6. Feeder, nipples, water. Penn State Extension feeder space is at least 3 in, so 15 × 3 = 45 in. Maryland Extension is 2-3 in, so 15 × 2 = 30 to 15 × 3 = 45 in. Penn State Extension says 1 nipple per 6-8 birds. At one nipple per 8 birds, 15 ÷ 8 = 1 with a remainder of 7, so round up to 2. At one nipple per 6 birds, 15 ÷ 6 = 2 with a remainder of 3, so round up to 3. Plan 2 to 3 nipples. Maryland Extension says a five-gallon waterer can accommodate up to 75 hens, and 15 is under 75, so 1 waterer covers the flock.
  7. Feed. Minnesota Extension says a 6-pound hen eats roughly 3 pounds of feed each week. 15 × 3 = 45 lb per week. That is 45/50 of a 50-lb bag per week.
  8. Example footprint. 60 sq ft is 6 × 10 ft. 150 sq ft is 10 × 15 ft.

FAQ

How big should a chicken coop be for 15 chickens?

Draw 60 sq ft of coop and 150 sq ft of run for 15 standard hens. That is 15 times 4 sq ft and 15 times 10 sq ft. The indoor 4 sq ft rate sits inside the eXtension 3-4 sq ft range and the Minnesota Extension 3 to 5 sq ft range.

Will one roost bar hold 15 hens?

Penn State's 6 in is 90 in, or 7 ft 6 in, which fits a 10 ft wall. Utah's upper 10 in is 150 in, or 12 ft 6 in, which is longer than a 10 ft wall, so that end of the range needs a second bar. Penn State spaces roosts 1 ft apart and Utah spaces bars 14 in apart.

How many nesting boxes for 15 hens?

15 divided by 5 is 3 boxes. 15 divided by 4 rounds up to 4 boxes. Penn State is 2 for the first 4 hens plus 3 more for the 11 additional hens, which is 5 nests.

What is the indoor minimum for 15 hens?

15 times 1.5 is 22.5 sq ft and 15 times 2 is 30 sq ft. That is the Penn State, Maryland, and Utah minimum band. eXtension says too little space leads to pecking and cannibalism, so 22.5 sq ft is a floor, not the plan.
