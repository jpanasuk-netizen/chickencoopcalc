How big should a chicken coop be?

Two numbers decide the build for standard birds. Coop interior — 4 sq ft per bird when they also have a run, which is the top of the 3–4 sq ft indoor range from eXtension and from the UC Master Gardener handout. Run — 10 sq ft per bird, the outdoor figure on both of those pages. Penn State and Maryland publish a lower indoor minimum (1.5 sq ft for laying hens, 2 sq ft for large chickens) and 8 or 10 sq ft of run. If the coop is the only space, give more floor than the 4 sq ft rate; those pages do not publish a "double it" rule. Bantams on the Penn State and Maryland tables are 1 sq ft inside and 4 sq ft of run. The floor plans at 4 and 10 work out to a 4×6 ft coop for 6 hens, an 8×8 for 12, and a 10×10 for 25.

Roost length is a separate constraint. Utah State Extension lists 6 to 10 inches of perch per bird, Penn State lists 6 inches for standard chickens, and Maryland lists at least 6 to 7 inches. At the 10-inch end of Utah's range, 12 hens need 10 ft of roost. Nests are separate: one per 4 to 5 hens on the UC, Maryland, and Utah pages. Hens still queue for a favorite box.

Common questions, answered

How many square feet does a chicken coop need per chicken?

eXtension recommends a minimum of 3–4 square feet indoors and 10 square feet outdoors per laying hen. The UC Master Gardener handout says about 3–4 square feet of enclosed coop and about 10 square feet of yard. This site uses 4 and 10. Penn State and Maryland list a lower indoor minimum and an 8 or 10 square foot run. Their bantam row is 1 square foot inside and 4 square feet of run; the calculator's 2 and 8 is extra room above that row. Six hens at 4 and 10 need a 24 sq ft coop (roughly 4×6 ft) and a 60 sq ft run.

How many nesting boxes do I need for my hens?

Published guidance is one nest per 4 to 5 hens. Six hens need two boxes; twelve need three. Penn State starts with two nests for the first four hens, then one more per four additional hens, and says the box should be at least 12×12 inches. This calculator counts one box per 3 hens, which is tighter than the published 4-to-5 range. The one-per-5 setting matches the wide end of that range.

How much feed does a chicken eat per day?

Alabama Extension says a laying hen eats about 0.25 lb of feed per day (100 to 150 grams) and lists layer diets at 16% protein or more. Kentucky Extension states the same quarter-pound rule of thumb. That is about 7.5 lb a month, or one 50 lb bag per hen every 6–7 weeks. At the default $22 per 50 lb bag, that is about $0.11 of feed per hen per day. Those pages do not give a percent for bantams or for freezing weather.

Do chickens need a heat lamp in the winter?

Maryland Extension starts chicks at 90–95°F the first week and drops 5°F each week until they are five weeks old, with the lamp at least 18 inches above the floor. The housing pages cited here do not publish an adult cutoff such as −20°F or a 250 W bulb size. Minnesota's cold-weather page does say to add supplemental heat when the coop falls below 35°F, and Maryland's winter fact sheet says dry, wind-protected adults should not need it. Both sentences, and the site's own 20°F planning line, are on the winterizing section.
