Best Chicken Coop to Keep Out Predators

What makes a coop predator-proof, by the numbers three extension services publish. No product picks; score the coop you have or the one you are looking at.

Built by Jeremy Panasuk. Published November 24, 2026.

Quick answer: The best chicken coop to keep out predators is raised, wrapped and dug in. Oregon State University Extension says to raise the coop at least 1 foot, enclose the run in 1- by 2-inch mesh with a covered top, and bury fencing 6 inches to 1 foot deep. Colorado State Extension warns bobcats and coyotes can clear 4-foot fences.

How to use the score

A coop that passes all six is raised, wrapped in small mesh, dug in and covered. Pick the mesh with our guide to what size hardware cloth is best, which also works out how much roll you need. Size the run first with the chicken run size calculator, and check floor space for your flock in the coop size by flock table.

Move roosts away from wire walls; set bar length with how much roost space per chicken. Every space figure we use is listed in the poultry space requirements data.

Mesh size and how many rolls to buy are on what size hardware cloth for a chicken coop.

Limits

The scorer checks the six numbers the extensions publish. It cannot see gaps under doors, rotten boards or a latch a raccoon can open. Oregon State also suggests a motion-detector light outside the coop.
