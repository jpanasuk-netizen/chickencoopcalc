How to feed baby chicks

The pounds of starter come from Maryland's feed table. The brooder floor comes from Maine. This site's feed and coop functions are for laying hens, and the worked example shows the number you get if you point them at chicks anyway.

Built by Jeremy Panasuk. Updated October 6, 2026.

Quick answer: Maryland Extension lists 4 lb of starter per brown-egg chick from day old to 6 weeks, and 3 lb per white-egg chick. Twelve brown-egg chicks are 48 lb of starter. The same 12 birds typed into this site's laying-hen feed function return 126 lb over 6 weeks, because that function uses 0.25 lb per bird per day. Do not feed them a layer ration. Maryland says the calcium is high enough to cause improper bone formation, kidney failure, and possibly death.

What to put in the feeder

Maryland's feeding guide says to use a commercial feed matched to the bird's type and age, and to use its amount table only as a guide. The amounts below are total feed for one bird, from Vest and Dale (2002), as printed in that guide.

Brown-egg layerDay old to 6 weeks4 lbStarter
Brown-egg layer7 to 18 weeks46 lbGrower
Brown-egg layer19 to 70 weeks104 lbLayer
White-egg layerDay old to 6 weeks3 lbStarter
White-egg layer7 to 18 weeks12 lbGrower
White-egg layer19 to 70 weeks80 lbLayer

Meat bird4 to 7 weeks7 lbFinisher

Maine's chick bulletin starts every chick on an all-mash starter for six weeks, then a finisher for meat birds or a grower/developer for other birds. Its protein bands are separate from Maryland's pound totals: pullets (future layers) get starter at 18–20% protein for weeks 0–6, grower at 15% for weeks 6–18, and layer feed at 17% protein from 18 weeks on. Broilers and fryers get starter at 22–23% protein for weeks 0–6 and finisher at 18–20% for weeks 6–8. Use fresh feed stored no longer than four weeks.

Utah State says layer-strain chicks get a 20–21% protein starter from 0 to 6 weeks, and it says not to feed a layer diet to chickens that are not in egg production, because the calcium is too high. Missouri says the same split: starting diets for young birds, a laying diet for mature hens, and problems from the wrong diet can show up quickly and are often irreversible.

Maryland says scratch grains are unnecessary on a complete diet and, if you feed them, should generally be about 10% of daily intake because they dilute the ration. An insoluble grit is part of that scratch sentence. Maine's first feed is the all-mash starter, not scratch.

Coccidiosis is the disease both Maryland and Maine flag for chicks on the ground. Maine says a medicated feed with a coccidiostat is the conventional approach in starter and grower only. Chicks vaccinated with Coccivac should get non-medicated feed, with no coccidiostat. Read the tag. Maryland says many coccidiostats have a withdrawal time before birds are marketed.

Floor, feeder, and water while they are small

Maine's space table, per bird:

AgeFloorFeederWaterer
0–4 weeks0.5 sq ft1.0 in0.5 in

8–12 weeks2.0 sq ft2.5 in1.0 in

For the first week to 10 days, Maine says you can feed in cut-down cardboard boxes with sides about 1¼ inches high, feed in piles. After 10 days, give enough feeder so every chick can eat at once, and keep feeders no more than half full. Fountain waterers: one quart jar per 16 chicks the first week, one quart per 20 chicks from one to four weeks old, and a one-gallon waterer per 20 chicks at 12 weeks. Clean the waterers every day.

Adult coop math is the wrong floor. calcCoopSize uses 4 sq ft indoors per standard bird. Twelve chicks would come back as 48 sq ft. Maine's first four weeks are 0.5 × 12 = 6 sq ft. The flock-size table is for the housing those birds need later, not for the brooder.

Heat, without borrowing a wattage

Maine measures temperature at chick level, 2 to 3 inches off the floor: 95°F the first week, then 5°F less each week until 70°F. Chicks huddled together are cold. Chicks spread under the heat are comfortable. Chicks staying away from it are too warm. They need fresh air without a draft. You may turn the brooder off on warm days.

Maryland's housing page, quoted on the winter heat page, starts the first week at 90–95°F and drops 5°F each week until the chicks are five weeks old and the room is about 70–75°F, with the lamp at least 18 inches above the floor. Utah State's basics sheet describes a different gradient: about 110°F under the heat source and 84°F at the edge of the brooder ring, decreased about 5°F a week, and it names a 250 watt infrared light as an example heat source. These are three published schedules. This page does not average 95°F and 110°F, and it does not assign Utah's 250 watt bulb to Maine's 6 sq ft floor.

Worked example: 12 brown-egg chicks

Maryland's starter total is the feed plan: 12 × 4 lb = 48 lb of starter from day old through 6 weeks. The same 12 white-egg chicks would be 12 × 3 = 36 lb. Meat birds are on a different row: 2 lb of starter each through 3 weeks, then 7 lb of finisher from 4 to 7 weeks.

Maine's first-month space for these 12: 12 × 0.5 = 6 sq ft of floor, 12 × 1.0 = 12 inches of feeder, 12 × 0.5 = 6 inches of waterer. Twelve is under 16, so one quart jar meets Maine's first-week fountain count of one jar per 16 chicks.

What the laying-hen functions return if you ignore that and type 12 anyway:

  • calcFeed(12, 6, 22) uses 0.25 lb per bird per day. Daily feed is 3 lb. Over 6 weeks the function rounds 3 × 7 × 6 to 126 lb, and at $22 per 50 lb bag the total cost field is $55.44. That 126 lb is a hen figure. It is not Maryland's 48 lb of starter.
  • calcCoopSize(12, "standard") returns 48 sq ft of coop and 120 sq ft of run. Maine's brooder floor for weeks 0–4 is 6 sq ft.
  • calcHeat(6, 40, true) is the brooder floor from Maine (6 sq ft) and a room temperature of 40°F, which is above this function's 20°F adult line, so adult watts are 0. Chick watts are the site ratio 250 W per 16 sq ft, capped at 16 sq ft and rounded up to the next 50 W: 6 × 250 / 16 = 93.75, which rounds to 100 W. Vent area on the site's 1-per-10 ratio, in half-foot steps, is 0.5 sq ft. Maine and Maryland do not publish that 100 W or that 0.5 sq ft.

Chicken brooder size

Maine Bulletin 2219 is the floor for a brooder, not the adult coop. Per bird: 0.5 sq ft for weeks 0–4, 1.0 sq ft for weeks 4–8, and 2.0 sq ft for weeks 8–12. The table above also lists feeder inches and waterer inches for those same bands. Twelve chicks in the first four weeks are 12 × 0.5 = 6 sq ft. calcCoopSize on those 12 returns 48 sq ft of coop, because that function is the laying-hen floor (4 sq ft). Do not brooder them on 48 sq ft and call it the Maine number.

The hen line is calcCoopSize for a standard bird. It is the adult mismatch, not the brooder answer. Heat-lamp temperatures stay on the winter page. This widget does not invent a wattage.

When to switch from starter to grower to layer

Maryland's table, cited to Vest and Dale 2002, is a pound total by stage, not a protein percent. Brown-egg birds: starter from day old to 6 weeks (4 lb), grower from 7 to 18 weeks (46 lb), layer from 19 to 70 weeks (104 lb). White-egg birds: 3 lb of starter, 12 lb of grower, and 80 lb of layer on those same week ranges. Meat birds leave starter at 3 weeks (2 lb) and eat finisher from 4 to 7 weeks (7 lb).

Maine's protein bands are a second schedule, not a conversion of those pounds. Pullets: starter at 18–20% protein for weeks 0–6, grower at 15% for weeks 6–18, and layer feed at 17% protein from 18 weeks on. Broilers and fryers: starter at 22–23% for weeks 0–6 and finisher at 18–20% for weeks 6–8. Utah State says layer-strain chicks get a 20–21% protein starter from 0 to 6 weeks, and not to feed a layer diet to chickens that are not in egg production. Do not average Maine's 18–20% with Utah's 20–21%.

Maryland says do not feed layer rations to younger birds. If a young chicken is fed a layer diet, the calcium level is high enough to cause improper bone formation, kidney failure, and possibly death. The laying-hen cost tool, once they are on that layer ration, is the feed cost page. calcFeed is that later bird. It is not the starter purchase.

Run your own count

Common mistake

Feeding layer pellets because the bag is already in the shed, or typing the chicks into the feed-cost calculator and buying 126 lb of layer feed for a group whose starter total is 48 lb. Maryland's warning on layer feed for a young chicken is bone and kidney damage, including death. The matching space mistake is brooding them on the adult coop floor from the flock table. Six square feet is the Maine number for 12 chicks in weeks 0–4. Forty-eight square feet is the later hen coop.

Limits, and when to call someone

Maryland says nutritional problems in a growing bird can appear quickly and are often irreversible, and that diagnostic labs usually see deficiency diseases such as curly toe paralysis, nutritional coryza, and rickets in birds on homemade rations. A chick that cannot stand, or a group that came from a non-commercial mix, is a veterinarian or extension call, not a ration you finish at home. Maine says coccidiosis is a common killer of chicks. Follow the feed tag on medicated starter, and use non-medicated feed when the chicks had Coccivac. Buy from an NPIP hatchery, which is Maine's instruction and also the first line of the USDA APHIS checklist on the new-bird page.

Heat-lamp clearance and fire warnings for a brooder lamp are on the winter heat page. Daily chick water in gallons, which Maine's inch table does not state, starts on the water page with Missouri's 3 gallons per 100 chicks at six weeks.
