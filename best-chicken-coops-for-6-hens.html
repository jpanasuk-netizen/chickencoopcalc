Best chicken coops for 6 hens (2026)

Short answer: whatever you buy, the inside needs 24 sq ft and the run 60 — and almost every prefab marketed as a "6-hen coop" fails that check. Here's the math, the money, and the spec sheet.

First, the number that disqualifies half the listings

Run the calculator: 6 standard birds → 24 sq ft coop, 60 sq ft run, 2 nesting boxes, ~5 ft of roost bar. Now look at the typical "$299 six-to-eight hen coop" listing: a 48"×40" footprint is 13.3 sq ft. That's 2.2 sq ft per bird — half the minimum. It will work for about four hens at a stretch, and the run attachment is usually another 10-15 sq ft of decorative chicken wire.

This is the most upvoted advice on r/BackYardChickens for a reason: ignore the bird count on the label and read the dimensions. Divide interior square feet by 4. If the answer is lower than the headline bird count, the listing is lying by a factor of 1.5-2.

Three ways to get there, priced

1. Prefab, done right ($600–1,200)

You're paying for a weekend saved. The good ones in this class are real 4×6 or larger walk-ins with proper vents. Check: plywood walls (not MDF/particle board, which swell and rot), asphalt or metal roof, actual latch hardware. Expect to reinforce it anyway — ship-flat prefab arrives with staples where there should be screws, and a raccoon finds every one.

2. Kit or DIY ($350–700 in materials)

A 4×6 coop is roughly 12 sheets' worth of project: 2×4s, one CDX plywood lift, one bundle of shingles or a metal panel, hardware cloth, pintle hinges, and two locks. It's the cheapest route per square foot and the only route that gets you the roost height, vent area, and deep-litter floor you want. Overbuilt beats clever — the coop needs to survive weather and predators, not win awards.

3. Buy the small coop anyway and add a run

The popular compromise on the subreddit: keep the prefab as the roost/lay box and attach a 10×6 hardware-cloth run built from 2×4s. The birds get their 60+ sq ft outside and only sleep inside, where 12 sq ft is still tight for 6 but workable for 4. Cheapest path to happy birds if you already own the small coop.

Then budget the other half of the hobby

The coop is the one-time cost; feed is the forever one. Six hens eat about 46 lb of layer feed a month — roughly one 50 lb bag — which the feed cost guide breaks down per flock size and per egg. Full monthly carrying costs for a 6-hen flock land around $25-35 in feed plus bedding; the coop pays for itself in skipped grocery eggs somewhere around year 3-5, and the entertainment pays for itself immediately.
