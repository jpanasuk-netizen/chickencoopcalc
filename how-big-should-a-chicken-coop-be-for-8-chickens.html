How Big Should a Chicken Coop Be for 8 Chickens?

Full breakdown of the 6-hen house this flock sits next to: how big a chicken coop should be for 6 hens.

Quick answer: Eight hens need 32 sq ft inside and 80 sq ft of run. Thirty-two is 8 × 4 sq ft and eighty is 8 × 10 sq ft, with the indoor 4 inside the eXtension 3-4 sq ft range and the Minnesota Extension 3 to 5 sq ft range. The 12 to 16 sq ft indoor minimum is a minimum, not a recommendation.

How to size it

Eight uses the same per-bird rates as the smaller flocks. The new constraint is bar length versus an 8 ft wall.

  1. Coop floor. 8 × 4 = 32 sq ft. eXtension publishes 3-4 sq ft indoors (8 × 3 = 24 to 8 × 4 = 32). Minnesota Extension publishes 3 to 5 sq ft (8 × 3 = 24 to 8 × 5 = 40). The 4 sq ft figure used on this site sits inside both ranges.
  2. Indoor minimum, not recommended. 8 × 1.5 = 12 sq ft and 8 × 2 = 16 sq ft. Penn State Extension lists 1.5 sq ft for laying hens and 2 sq ft for large chickens, Maryland Extension prints the same table, and Utah State Extension lists 1.5 to 2.0 sq ft. Stay above that floor.
  3. Run. 8 × 10 = 80 sq ft from eXtension. Penn State Extension and Maryland Extension also list 10 sq ft of run for large chickens. Their laying-hen run line is 8 sq ft, and 8 × 8 = 64 sq ft.
  4. Nests, two published counts. Minnesota Extension, Utah State Extension, and Maryland Extension say one nest per 4 to 5 hens. One per 5: 8 ÷ 5 = 1 with a remainder of 3, so round up to 2. One per 4: 8 ÷ 4 = 2. Both ends land on 2 boxes. Penn State Extension starts with 2 nests for the first 4 hens, then one more per 4 additional hens. 8 − 4 = 4 additional hens, and 4 ÷ 4 = 1, so add 1. The total is 3 nests.
  5. Roost length. Utah State Extension 6 to 10 in is 48 in (4 ft) to 80 in (6 ft 8 in). Penn State Extension 6 in is 48 in (4 ft). Maryland Extension 6 to 7 in is 48 in (4 ft) to 56 in (4 ft 8 in). Divide inches by 12 to get feet.
  6. Feeder, nipples, water. Penn State Extension feeder space is at least 3 in, so 8 × 3 = 24 in. Maryland Extension is 2-3 in, so 8 × 2 = 16 to 8 × 3 = 24 in. Penn State Extension says 1 nipple per 6-8 birds. At one nipple per 8 birds, 8 ÷ 8 = 1. At one nipple per 6 birds, 8 ÷ 6 = 1 with a remainder of 2, so round up to 2. Plan 1 to 2 nipples. Maryland Extension says a five-gallon waterer can accommodate up to 75 hens, and 8 is under 75, so 1 waterer covers the flock.
  7. Feed. Minnesota Extension says a 6-pound hen eats roughly 3 pounds of feed each week. 8 × 3 = 24 lb per week. That is 24/50 of a 50-lb bag per week.
  8. Example footprint. 32 sq ft is 4 × 8 ft. 80 sq ft is 8 × 10 ft.

On the openings, Maryland Extension says to use 1/2-inch hardware cloth over windows. On the run skirt, Penn State Extension says to bury run wire at least 12 in deep and toe it outward about 6 in.

FAQ

How big should a chicken coop be for 8 chickens?

Build 32 sq ft of coop and 80 sq ft of run for 8 standard hens. That is 8 times 4 sq ft and 8 times 10 sq ft. The indoor 4 sq ft rate sits inside the eXtension 3-4 sq ft range and the Minnesota Extension 3 to 5 sq ft range.

What is the bare indoor minimum for 8 hens?

8 times 1.5 is 12 sq ft and 8 times 2 is 16 sq ft. Those rates come from Penn State and Maryland (1.5 for laying hens, 2 for large chickens) and from Utah (1.5 to 2.0). The range is a minimum, not a recommendation.

How many nesting boxes for 8 hens?

8 divided by 4 is 2, and 8 divided by 5 rounds up to 2, so the one-per-4-to-5 rule is 2 boxes. Penn State is 2 nests for the first 4 hens plus 1 more for the next 4, which is 3 nests.

Does a heavier hen change the 3 lb feed figure?

Minnesota publishes roughly 3 pounds a week for a 6-pound hen, and lists Rhode Island Red and Wyandotte hens at about 6.5 lb and Orpingtons at about 8 lb. It does not publish a different weekly amount for those weights, so 8 hens are still 24 lb a week on the 6-pound rate.
