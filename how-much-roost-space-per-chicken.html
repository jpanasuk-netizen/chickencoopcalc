How Much Roost Space Per Chicken?

The whole house for six hens, roost included, is the 6-hen coop page. This page is only the bar.

Quick answer: Give each standard chicken 6 to 10 in of perch (Utah State Extension), 6 in (Penn State Extension), or 6 to 7 in (Maryland Extension). Bantams are 4 in on the Penn State Extension line. Divide the inches by 12 when you need feet.

Length, height, and spacing are three different measurements, and the sources do not collapse them into one number. A 6 in Penn State roost can still be wrong if it is 5 ft off the floor, because Penn State Extension says never more than 4 ft, and Utah State Extension says no more than about 3 ft with 12 in of clearance under the perch. Maryland Extension wants the roost higher than the nest boxes and gives both nests and roosts at 24 in off the floor, plus a bar at least 2 in in diameter.

Bantams do not inherit the 6 in. Penn State Extension cuts them to 4 in and drops the height to 12-18 in. Ten bantams are worked in the breed-size section. Large meaty breeds get no perches at all on the Penn State page, so do not saw a bar for that row.

How to size it

Pick the bird type first. Standard hens have three published lengths. Bantams have one. Large meaty breeds have a published refusal.

  1. Standard length. Multiply the head count by 6 in (Penn State Extension), by 6 and by 7 in (Maryland Extension), and by 6 and by 10 in (Utah State Extension). Divide each product by 12.
  2. Six hens, as a check. 6 × 6 = 36 in, which is 3 ft. 6 × 10 = 60 in, which is 5 ft. 6 × 7 = 42 in, which is 3 ft 6 in.
  3. Height. Standard bars go 18-24 in up and stop at 4 ft (Penn State Extension). Utah stops near 3 ft and wants 12 in clear underneath. Bantam bars go 12-18 in up.
  4. Spacing and shape. Leave 1 ft between roosts (Penn State Extension) or 14 in between bars (Utah State Extension). Use a 2x2 with rounded tops (Utah State Extension) or a bar at least 2 in in diameter (Maryland Extension).
  5. When one wall is too short. The 15-hen example shows a case where 12 ft 6 in of Utah roost is longer than the 10 ft wall of that example coop. Add a second bar at the spacing above instead of shortening the inches.

Bumblefoot

The Merck Veterinary Manual's poultry chapter on staphylococcosis says bumblefoot (pododermatitis) is more common in heavier birds and in males. Swollen footpads and limping are common signs. It is often precipitated by injuries that let contamination into the subcutaneous tissue of the footpad. Prevention on that page is about those injuries: eliminate wood splinters in the litter, protruding wires, fighting, and cannibalism, and use good litter management to control footpad injuries. Diagnosis is confirmed by culture. Staphylococcus is common in the environment, so finding the bacteria is not by itself the diagnosis.

A second Merck chapter, on backyard poultry management, says wet litter leads to footpad dermatitis. Ideally litter contains 20–25% moisture: grab a handful and it should clump briefly and then crumble. Excessively dry litter, such as sawdust, is the dusty case on that page, not the footpad case. Wood flooring can leave exposed splinters, which that chapter lists as a cause of injuries.

Perch height is a different measurement, already in the tables above. Utah says perches should not be more than about 3 feet off the ground, or there may be more bruising of the feet or ruptured eggs as hens mount the roost. That sentence is about mounting the bar. The poultry staphylococcosis chapter does not list perch height as a cause of bumblefoot. A pet-bird page about padded perches is not a chicken instruction, and this page does not apply it.

Call a veterinarian. Merck says staphylococcosis can be treated with antimicrobials after a susceptibility test, given in the drinking water according to the label, with egg and meat withdrawal periods. It also says antimicrobial treatment is more successful for systemic infection than for localized infection. This page does not give a dose, a drug to buy, or a way to lance a foot. A swollen footpad or a limping bird is a veterinary call.

FAQ

How much roost space per chicken?

Utah says 6 to 10 inches of linear perch per chicken. Penn State says 6 inches for standard chickens and 4 inches for bantams. Maryland says at least 6 to 7 inches. Divide by 12 to convert inches to feet.

How high should a chicken roost be?

Penn State places standard roosts 18-24 inches above the floor and never more than 4 ft off the floor. Bantam roosts on that page are 12-18 inches above the floor. Utah says perches no more than about 3 ft high, with at least 12 inches of clearance under them.

How far apart should roost bars be?

Penn State spaces roosts 1 ft apart. Utah spaces perch bars 14 inches apart. Maryland says roosts should be higher than the nest boxes, and it places nests and roosts 24 inches above the floor.

What lumber works as a roost?

Utah says a 2x2 with rounded tops is ideal. Maryland says roosts at least 2 inches in diameter. Penn State says not to give perches to large meaty breeds.
